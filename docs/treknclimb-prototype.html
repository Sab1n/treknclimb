Before you come

Visa

On arrival at Kathmandu for most nationalities. 15, 30 or 90 days, paid in cash or card at the airport. Bring a passport photo to save time.

Best season

Late September to November is the most stable. March to May is warmer with hazier views and rhododendron in bloom. We run winter trips at lower altitude only.

Permits

TIMS card plus the relevant national park or conservation area entry. Restricted areas such as Upper Mustang and Manaslu need an additional permit and a licensed guide. All of this is in your quoted price.

India requires a visa before arrival for most nationalities. The e-visa covers tourism and is usually issued in three to five working days.

What you need

Permits

Every trek needs a TIMS card. National park or conservation area fees vary by region and are included in our prices. Restricted areas require a licensed guide by law — not an upsell, an actual regulation.

Fitness

Build up to walking six hours with a daypack over consecutive days. Stair sessions and loaded hill walks in the three months before you fly matter more than gym cardio.

Gear

Boots broken in over at least 50 km, a four-season sleeping bag (we rent these in Pokhara), layers rather than one heavy jacket, and a duffel your porter can carry. We send a full list on booking.

Everest Base Camp Trek

14days
5,364metres max
Moderategrade
2–12group size
Altitude by day
Day 1 · LuklaDay 9 · Base campDay 14 · Lukla
The Everest Base Camp trek costs $1,395 per person and takes 14 days from Lukla and back. It reaches a maximum altitude of 5,364 m at base camp and 5,545 m at Kala Patthar. It is graded moderate: no climbing skill is needed, but you walk 5 to 7 hours on most days. The best months are late September to November and March to May.

Overview

This is the classic route into the Khumbu, following the Dudh Koshi from Lukla to Namche Bazaar, then up through Tengboche and Dingboche to Gorak Shep and base camp itself. We build in two acclimatisation days rather than one, which is the single biggest factor in whether people summit Kala Patthar feeling well or feeling wrecked.

You sleep in teahouses throughout. A porter carries the main duffel; you walk with a daypack. Group size is capped at twelve, above which we add a second guide.

Highlights

  • Two acclimatisation days at Namche and Dingboche, not one
  • Sunrise from Kala Patthar at 5,545 m, the classic view of the Everest south face
  • Tengboche monastery, the largest gompa in the Khumbu
  • Sherpa museum at Namche and a rest-day walk to Khumjung
  • Flights to and from Lukla included, with a contingency day built in

Travel tips

Altitude sickness: what our guides actually watch for

Published 14 Jul 2026 · Updated 03 Sep 2026 · 7 min read · Bishnu Gurung

Every guide we employ has turned a group around at some point. It is the least popular decision in the job and the only one that matters. Here is what we are watching for, and what you should tell us even when you would rather not.

The three-symptom rule

Acute mountain sickness starts as a headache that paracetamol does not fully clear, plus any one of nausea, dizziness, unusual breathlessness at rest or broken sleep. One symptom on its own is common and usually resolves. Two or more that persist through a rest day is our threshold for descent.

The reason we ask about sleep every morning is that poor sleep is the earliest reliable signal, and the one people dismiss as jet lag or an uncomfortable teahouse mattress.

Why our itineraries look slower than the competition

A twelve-day Everest Base Camp itinerary exists and people sell it. It works for perhaps seven in ten trekkers. Our fourteen-day version adds acclimatisation days at Namche and Dingboche, which moves that number close to nine in ten. Two days is a small price for the difference between reaching base camp and being evacuated from Pheriche.

What we carry

  • A pulse oximeter, checked every evening above 3,500 m
  • Acetazolamide, for guide-supervised use only
  • Satellite phone on restricted-area routes
  • Evacuation contacts pre-arranged with the insurer before departure

The descent rule

Descent is the only reliable treatment. Oxygen and medication buy time to descend; they are not a substitute for it. If a guide calls it, the group does not vote.

How this started

Bishnu Gurung guided for other companies for eight seasons before starting this one, mostly because he was tired of explaining to clients why the itinerary they bought in Europe did not match the mountain they were standing on.

The company has stayed deliberately small. We turn down more work than we take, because the alternative is handing groups to agencies we have not vetted. Every guide who meets you at the airport is on our payroll, insured by us, and has walked your route before.

When you send an inquiry we collect your name, email address, phone number, preferred travel dates, group size and whatever you write in the message field. We do not collect payment details through this website — there is no checkout.
